Self Assessment for Locum Optometrists: Session Fees, Travel and Professional Fees

A locum optometrist's year is a rota of other people's practices: two days here, a week there, domiciliary visits in between. Self Assessment is where the rota is added up.

Session income

Every session is income when the work is done, at the agreed day or session rate — NHS and private testing alike, since the practice pays you the fee either way. Agency placements show the gross fee and the agency's commission: the gross is the income, the commission a cost. Keep every remittance; the return is built from them.

Travel between practices

Mileage to changing practices and one-off cover is a working cost, claimed by one consistent method with a log. A long regular block at one practice starts to look like a commute — the pattern in the diary is what decides, so keep the diary accurate. Domiciliary rounds are travel by design: visits, mileage and parking belong together in the record.

Kit and professional costs

Your own trial frame, retinoscope and other kit are equipment — keep the invoices. GOC registration, indemnity and CET courses are the ordinary costs of staying registered. If you also hold an employed post, that pay is declared separately as employment income alongside the locum pages.

The deadline

File online and pay by 31 January 2027 for 2025/26. A rota diary kept through the year turns the return into a summary instead of a reconstruction.
